## What the existing new metrics change

The completed 32-run replay preserves every original strict and secondary witness. On natural16 acquisition, Luna1024 has 51.54% content recall versus 20.21% at 640, while strict-or-equivalent complete tables remain four in both. That suggests an acquisition/coverage difference, rather than treating every low strict cell count as a semantic transcription failure. This is one retained run at each setting, not a resolution guarantee.

On random40, Haiku640 recovers content for all 36 expected tables versus 33 strict complete tables, but the three differing span topologies remain incorrect as schema, and its 108-unit negative-page extra table lowers precision. Luna640 has aggregate content precision/recall of 100%, but an extra blank row/altered span prevents one table from being content-complete. Neither observation permits full-structure credit from recovered text alone. The frozen confirmation48 results likewise remain an audit of existing outputs, not a source of new prompt labels.

The next useful comparison is therefore selected-only content precision and full-cohort recall for the three decision families above. It can distinguish abstention/coverage loss, real literal loss and schema disagreement without crediting a retained fallback. Candidate and ownership models must remain separate; header hypotheses reuse generated body strings and cannot be called source-conserved correction.

## Interpretation and bounded next experiments

PPLX128 RGB has selected-only content precision/recall of 94.62%/55.85%, versus 92.59%/3.46% for the matched blank-image control. The blank arm’s high precision concerns very little emitted content; full-cohort recall exposes the coverage collapse. PPLX512 RGB and palette share 95.13%/61.22%, with five strict complete tables and six strict-or-equivalent tables. The companion adds one spacing-equivalent table rather than certifying widespread alternative columns. The 400 RGB/seed19 arm has higher strict cells but 92.94%/60.68% content P/R and three extra tables. Edge and alias ordering both differ, so that comparison is not causal.

Ownership adds no schema-equivalent complete table across all 20 arms. Hybrid Jev has 82.15%/21.44% content P/R; hybrid PPLX has 85.32%/18.75%. These are completed compositions including deterministic geometry and protected source, with failed/skipped pages removed from quality credit. The new diagnostic recovers partial literal credit but does not reveal an unnamed-column equivalence that rescues complete tables. A cell-only strict leaderboard would hide this precision/coverage tradeoff.

Header hypotheses can retain substantial supplied body text despite zero complete tables: PPLX full512 and Jev full text both have 87.82%/18.72% full-cohort content P/R. None of the 20 arms is content-complete or schema-equivalent for a matched table. These four menus cover one failed page, so the low full-cohort recall cannot be interpreted as a standalone all-page extraction ability. No fixed historical tables or unseen replacement strings receive credit.

1. Confirm the PPLX128/512 selector precision–coverage tradeoff using a predeclared paired repeat on a new authorized producer-disjoint DEV cohort and fixed candidate menus/alias order. Retain blank evidence as a falsification control, all abstentions and all negative pages. Compare selected-only precision, full-cohort recall, complete tables and actual incremental cost separately. This is a proposal, not a launch or fresh-confirmation retuning.
2. Test source-owned row/column proposals that alter the topology language rather than another prompt over the same fixed assignments. Freeze proposed boundaries and unresolved source IDs before references; measure bounded candidate content headroom before paying for selection. The absence of additional schema-complete ownership tables cautions against claiming that scorer tolerance alone fixes the mechanism.
3. For generated header alternatives, expose row-order/body omission witnesses and validate complete candidate content before asking a model to rank headers. Keep the existing full versus local-header treatment distinction and original zero-complete failures. A future finite pool should test recoverable full content and spans, with oracle diagnostics separate from actual choices; it should not copy reference text into alternatives.
