# Incremental financial ledger index: design only

An incremental index could reduce repeated full-history parsing while retaining one shared financial authority. A second independent spending cap or a cache trusted without recovery would weaken the existing contract. No implementation, migration, policy activation, or live ledger access is proposed here.

One possible design keeps the existing ledger as the authoritative checkpoint and adds an append-only, versioned transaction journal plus a derived index. Each reservation, settlement, and annotation receives a monotonically increasing sequence, exact attempt identity, payload digest, and previous-record digest. Under the existing interprocess lock, reservation checks consult a verified index covering the checkpoint and every committed journal record. The journal write must reach durable storage before admission returns and before a provider request can begin. Reservation entries remain unknown until an authenticated settlement is durable; stopped or crashed callers never imply zero charge. Settlement is unique per attempt, and annotation cannot revise financial settlement.

The index retains cumulative and per-wave positive charges, labeled estimates, unknown reservations, all in-flight reservations, request/tag indexes, and settled attempt identities. Admission continues to enforce the sole cumulative cap, wave cap, deadlines, concurrency limits, matched-replicate rules, failed-request blocking, and model/provider controls. Different processes cannot independently reserve against stale totals. A corrupt, incomplete, or unrecognized index triggers reconstruction under the lock or fail-closed refusal.

Checkpoint compaction writes a complete temporary ledger and index with a journal sequence boundary, fsyncs both, atomically publishes a versioned manifest, and fsyncs its directory. Old generations and the journal remain available until recovery proves the new generation complete. Recovery selects a complete manifest, verifies journal continuity and digests, replays committed transactions exactly once, and refuses ambiguous identities or missing committed records. A provider response lost before settlement leaves the original reservation unknown; reconciliation remains an explicit root operation, rather than an automatic retry.

The next offline experiment should compare the actual current transaction flow against this indexed design on synthetic histories, including multi-process admission races and injected crashes after every durability boundary. Required invariants include identical cap decisions and final sums, no duplicate settlement, no duplicate transmission, no dropped unknown reservation, and deterministic recovery. Measure lock hold and wait separately, durable write counts, journal/checkpoint bytes, CPU/RSS/wall, and telemetry occupancy. SQLite's favorable synthetic result and the guarded codec's negative result justify investigating indexing; neither establishes production safety or a deployable migration.
